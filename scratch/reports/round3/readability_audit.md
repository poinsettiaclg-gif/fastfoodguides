# Readability Audit Report - Round 3

**Date:** July 31, 2026
**Auditor:** Readability Auditor

## Objective
Review 5 random articles in the `fastfoodguides.com` project for "walls of text." Assess whether paragraphs are short and digestible, and if headers are used effectively.

## Sample Evaluated
1. `applebees-microwave-reality.md`
2. `chick-fil-a-core-4.md`
3. `dairy-queen-perfect-cone-curl.md`
4. `five-guys-burger-build.md`
5. `mcdonalds-fresh-beef-grill-process.md`

## Findings

Overall, the readability across the sampled articles is excellent. There are no severe "walls of text," and the content is formatted with online readability best practices in mind.

### 1. Header Usage (Effective)
- **H2 and H3 Tags:** All 5 articles use `##` (H2) and `###` (H3) headers effectively to create a clear structural hierarchy.
- **Sectioning:** Content is logically grouped. For example, `applebees-microwave-reality.md` breaks down its content into specific categories like "Sides," "Soups," and "Desserts," making it incredibly scannable. `five-guys-burger-build.md` uses headers to walk the reader through chronological steps (e.g., "Step 1: Ball to Grill").

### 2. Paragraph Digestibility
- **Short Paragraphs:** The vast majority of paragraphs fall within the ideal 2-4 sentence range. This prevents visual fatigue and makes the content highly digestible on mobile devices.
- **Exceptions:** The introductory paragraph in `chick-fil-a-core-4.md` is slightly longer (around 5-6 sentences) but still remains acceptable and engaging.
- **Formatting Elements:** Articles frequently utilize bulleted/numbered lists and `callout-tip` blocks (`ProTip`). These elements naturally break up the text flow and prevent any potential text walls.

## Conclusion
The current formatting guidelines are being successfully implemented. The use of headers is highly effective, and paragraphs remain short and punchy. No structural code changes or rewrites are recommended for these files based on readability.
