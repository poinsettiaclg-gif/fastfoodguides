# AdSense Policy Audit: Subway through Wendy's

**Auditor:** Extremely Critical AdSense Policy Auditor
**Date:** 2026-08-01

## Executive Summary

After reviewing the articles in the `subway` through `wendys` categories, I have found numerous red flags that could trigger Google AdSense rejections under their "Thin Content" and "Low Value Content" policies. Many articles exhibit severe grammatical errors, AI-spun content structures, and a distinct lack of deep operational expertise despite attempting to adopt the "kitchen manager" persona.

Here are the brutal, unfiltered findings:

### 1. Severe Grammatical Errors & Typos
There are blatant typos and grammatical mistakes that make the content look incredibly unprofessional and amateurish. For instance, in `subway-bain-fill-line-rule.md`:
- "But tthis is one strict health-code rule"
- "it happen on a routine inspection"
- "The critical principle this is swap, not top off."
- "Tthis is no workaround for this one"

These look like a malfunctioning AI generator or extremely careless editing. Google's quality raters will immediately flag this as low-quality content.

### 2. Spun Content Patterns & Repetitive Structures
While the exact banned phrases were largely avoided, the pacing and transitions are highly formulaic. Many articles use the exact same structural rhythm:
- A generic intro establishing the "importance" of a machine.
- A "How it actually works" section.
- A "Common Mistakes" section.
- A FAQ.

While structured correctly per the markdown rules, the *prose* feels like it was spun from the same template. It lacks the varied, gritty tone of a real kitchen manager. 

### 3. Keyword Stuffing
Several articles attempt to force their primary keywords too frequently. For example, the `wendys-frosty-machine-boil-out.md` and `taco-bell-baja-blast.md` articles repeat the name of the machine and the brand name in unnatural ways throughout the headings and body text, reading more like an SEO grab than an authentic operational guide.

### 4. Lack of Operational Expertise
Some of the "ProTips" are too generic. Instead of providing deep, proprietary workflow secrets, they regurgitate standard food safety rules (e.g., "The open-air unit is designed to hold temperatures, not lower them"). A real 10-year kitchen manager would share gritty, specific details like how to clean a specific jammed gasket or deal with a broken timer sensor.

## Verdict
**REJECT.** The current batch needs a heavy editorial pass to fix the embarrassing typos (e.g., "Tthis") and to inject actual, gritty operational details rather than surface-level food safety manual regurgitations. Stop relying on formulaic spun structures.
