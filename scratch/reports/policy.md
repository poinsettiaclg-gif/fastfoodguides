# AdSense Policy Review: High-Risk Articles

## Overview
A comprehensive review of the `src/content/articles` directory was conducted to identify any content that might violate Google AdSense policies. The review specifically targeted:
1. Derogatory language (hate speech, slurs, extreme profanity)
2. Illegal acts (promotion of fraud, violence, theft, drug use)
3. Extreme brand bashing or libelous claims against fast-food chains

## Findings

**No AdSense policy violations were found in the reviewed articles.**

### Specific Topics Reviewed:
- **Lawsuits and Controversies**: Articles covering historical controversies (e.g., `subway-tuna.md`, `mcdonalds-ice-cream-machine.md`, `mcdonalds-fry-station.md`) discuss these events objectively. They report established facts without promoting illegal acts or engaging in extreme brand bashing. For instance, `subway-tuna.md` actively debunks the "fake tuna" lawsuit and defends the brand.
- **Employee Safety and Practices**: Articles discussing potential hazards (e.g., `pizza-delivery-driver-accident.md`, `kfc-pressure-fryers.md`) emphasize following corporate safety protocols and the law (such as explicitly advising against insurance fraud), rather than promoting illegal acts.
- **Derogatory Language**: A comprehensive search for extreme profanity, slurs, and hate speech returned no results. The language used remains professional, instructional, and aligned with the intended gritty, realistic kitchen manager persona without crossing into AdSense violations. 
- **Brand Criticism**: While some articles critique specific operational workflows or equipment (e.g., criticizing the UI of the McDonald's Taylor ice cream machines as "terrible"), these critiques are grounded in operational analysis and do not constitute "extreme brand bashing" or libel that would trigger an AdSense penalty.

## Conclusion
The content maintains a safe, educational, and compliant standard suitable for AdSense monetization while adhering to the specified persona constraints.
