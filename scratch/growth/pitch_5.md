# Article Pitch: The Friday Night Shortage (Surviving a 10-Case Fry Deficit)

**Target Audience:** General Managers and Multi-Unit Leaders actively looking for inventory management software.
**Search Intent:** High-intent. GMs bleeding food cost and dealing with inventory variance nightmares who want a systematic fix.

## Concept
It's 2 PM on a Friday. The weekend rush is looming. You check the walk-in and realize the morning truck delivery shorted you 10 cases of fries, but the invoice says they were delivered. This article walks through the immediate operational chaos of this scenario—borrowing product from a sister store, adjusting prep pars on the fly, and the eventual realization that manual inventory tracking is costing the store thousands a month. It naturally pivots into why automated inventory and variance tracking software is the only way to catch delivery shorts before the driver leaves the lot.

## Proposed Structure (Meeting Site Guidelines)
- **Introduction**: Gritty recount of the panic when a core item is shorted before peak volume. The reality of the line when the math doesn't add up.
- **Immediate Triage**: Sending a shift lead in their personal car to beg for boxes from another location. The disruption to the shift workflow.
- **The Post-Mortem**: Finding the variance. Why manual invoice reconciliation fails when managers are rushed and just sign the clipboard to get the driver out the back door.
- **The Software Fix**: How inventory platforms flag invoice-to-delivery discrepancies instantly, forcing accountability on the supplier before the truck pulls away.
- **ProTip Components**: 
  - *ProTip 1*: Always blind-receive core items like fries, protein, and oil. 
  - *ProTip 2*: Set variance alerts on your BOH software so multi-unit managers get pinged the moment an invoice doesn't match the PO.
- **FAQ Schema**:
  - *Question 1*: How do you handle a shorted delivery after the truck leaves?
  - *Question 2*: What is the acceptable inventory variance for QSR fries?

## Tone
Direct, tired, and experienced. Written from the perspective of someone who has spent hours staring at a clipboard in a freezing walk-in, sick of vendor mistakes eating into store margins.
