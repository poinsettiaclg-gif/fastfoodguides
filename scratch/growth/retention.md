# Retention Strategy: "Weekly Shift Notes"

## 1. Concept & Persona
**Name**: "Weekly Shift Notes" (Alt: "The BOH Bulletin", "Close-to-Open")
**Sender**: Russell Roseberry (10-year QSR kitchen manager)
**Tone**: Gritty, practical, no-fluff. Written like a shift handover log or a quick huddle in the walk-in. It should feel like a veteran manager giving a heads-up on the coming week's chaos and sharing hard-earned operational shortcuts. 

## 2. Capture Strategy (How we get emails)
Fast food workers and managers are busy and naturally skeptical of corporate-speak. We need high-value, highly specific lead magnets.
*   **Lead Magnet 1: "The Closing Shift Cheat Sheet"** - A printable checklist for efficient closing procedures, targeting night-shift managers who want to get their crew out 30 minutes faster.
*   **Lead Magnet 2: "Line Check Mastery"** - A guide on passing EcoSure/health audits without losing your mind.
*   **Capture Points**:
    *   Inline sign-up boxes styled as a "Manager's Logbook" at the bottom of every article.
    *   Exit-intent popups on high-traffic operational guides.
    *   A dedicated `/shift-notes` landing page linked in the site's main navigation.

## 3. Content Strategy (What we send)
Send once a week (e.g., Sunday morning, as managers are prepping for the week). Keep it brief, scan-friendly, and mobile-optimized.
*   **The "Shift Handover"**: A short intro from Russell about a real-world restaurant problem (e.g., dealing with a broken fryer during a rush, or navigating a poorly planned corporate LTO rollout).
*   **Pro Tip of the Week**: One actionable operational shortcut (e.g., how to organize the line for a $1,000 hour).
*   **Featured Guides**: Link to 1-2 new or cornerstone articles on `fastfoodguides.com` (e.g., "Breakfast Transition Guide", "Drive-Thru Timer Hacks").
*   **The Walk-In Rant**: A relatable, humorous venting section about typical fast-food headaches (call-outs, truck order mistakes) to build community and empathy.

## 4. Technical Execution
*   **Platform**: Substack (for community features/comments) or MailerLite (for cheap, easy automations).
*   **Design**: Plain text or minimal HTML. It should look like an actual email from a manager, not a polished marketing blast.
*   **Welcome Sequence**: 
    1.  *Day 0*: "Welcome to the Line + Your Download"
    2.  *Day 2*: "The biggest mistake I made as a new kitchen manager" (links to a cornerstone article).
    3.  *Day 5*: "What chain are you working at right now?" (encourages replies to boost email deliverability and gather audience data).
