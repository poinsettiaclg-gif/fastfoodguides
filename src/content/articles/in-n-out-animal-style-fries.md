While [In-N-Out Burger](/articles/in-n-out-board-station) is famous for having one of the simplest printed menus in fast food—offering only burgers, fries, shakes, and drinks—any West Coast QSR manager knows that the kitchen operates on an entirely different plane due to the **Secret Menu**.

The undisputed volume leader of this off-menu ecosystem is **Animal Style Fries**. When a customer orders this loaded dish, it triggers a complex cross-station workflow that requires total synchronization between the fry cook, the burger grill cook, and the Board assembly worker. 

In a kitchen processing over 100 tickets per hour, integrating a multi-layered gourmet fry build into a standard QSR assembly line is an operational challenge. This is how In-N-Out builds Animal Style Fries at scale.

## 1. The Kennebec Potato Base: The Single-Fry Reality

To understand Animal Style Fries, you must first understand the structural reality of standard In-N-Out fries. While almost every major fast-food chain (such as [McDonald's](/articles/mcdonalds-fry-station) and [Five Guys](/articles/five-guys-fry-calibration)) utilizes a two-stage cooking process—blanching potatoes at a lower temperature before flash-frying at a high temperature—In-N-Out utilizes a **live, in-store single-fry system**.

### Whole Potato Slicing and Sunflower Oil
Every morning, prep workers load whole, unpeeled **Kennebec potatoes** into manual wall-mounted dicing presses. The raw potato sticks fall directly into sinks of cold water to rinse off surface starches before being spun dry in commercial salad spinners.
*   **The Fryer Drop:** When an order for Animal Style Fries prints on the kitchen printer (ticketed simply as `ANML FRY`), the fry cook drops a portion of fresh-cut potatoes into 100% cholesterol-free sunflower oil maintained at **395°F for roughly 5 minutes**.
*   **Why This Matters for Animal Style:** Because single-fried Kennebec potatoes have a delicate, lacy crust rather than a thick, glassy starch shell, they are inherently prone to rapid softening. This makes the assembly timing of Animal Style Fries critical; if the fries sit in the boat waiting for cheese for even 60 seconds, they will collapse into mush.

## 2. The Red Boat and The 2-Slice Cheese Layout

<div class="callout callout-tip">**ProTip:** The term "All the Way" is often used internally by employees to refer to standard Animal Style fries (spread and grilled onions). If a customer wants modifications, the fry station associate must verbally coordinate with the board and grill stations.</div>

Once the fries are drained and salted with fine-grain salt, the cook transfers them into a dedicated **red paperboard tray** (known internally as a "boat" or "large red"). Unlike standard fry bags, the red boat provides a wide, rigid horizontal surface required for multi-topping layering.

### The American Cheese Placement
The first topping layer is always the American cheese. 
*   The worker takes two full slices of high-melt commercial American cheese and lays them side-by-side across the top of the hot fries.
*   **Coverage Rule:** The slices must overlap slightly in the center while extending nearly to the edges of the cardboard boat, ensuring that when melted, every single forkful of fries yields a cheese pull.

## 3. The Flat-Top Steaming Hack: The 20-Second Melt

This is where In-N-Out's operational genius shines. How do you melt two thick slices of cheese across a basket of fries without installing a slow, space-wasting cheese melter or salamander oven in an already crowded kitchen? **You use the burger grill.**

### The Stainless Steel Dome Technique
The fry cook hands the prepared red boat directly across the aisle to the burger grill cook working the 400°F chrome flat-top griddle.
1.  **Grill Edge Placement:** The grill cook places the paperboard boat directly onto a clean, slightly cooler edge zone of the griddle (around 350°F to 375°F).
2.  **The Splash and Cover:** Using a squirt bottle, the cook squirts half an ounce of water directly onto the bare metal griddle immediately adjacent to the cardboard boat, then covers the entire boat with a heavy, rectangular **stainless steel melting dome**.
3.  **Thermodynamic Steam Sealing:** The trapped water vapor instantly converts into high-pressure steam inside the dome. In exactly **15 to 20 seconds**, the convective steam heat liquefies the American cheese into a glossy, blanketed emulsion over the hot fries without scorching the bottom of the cardboard boat.

<div class="callout callout-tip">**ProTip:** Because Animal Style toppings inject significant moisture into the potato base, veteran customers and employees often order their Animal Style Fries **"Light Well"** or **"Well Done."** The fry cook leaves the potatoes in the fryer for an extra 90 to 120 seconds, driving out internal water and creating a crispier texture that holds up under the heavy toppings.</div>

## 4. The Final Build: Spread and Grilled Onions

The moment the melting dome is lifted, the cheese-covered boat is handed back to the Board worker for final condiment dressing.

### The Spread Dollop
Using the same offset spatula or squeeze bottle used for dressing Double-Doubles, the worker applies a heavy, zigzagging ribbon of In-N-Out's proprietary **Thousand Island-style spread** directly across the melted cheese. 

### The Grilled Onion Cap
The final crowning touch is a generous scoop of **caramelized grilled onions**. 
*   Unlike raw onions or lightly sautéed onions, In-N-Out's grilled onions are cooked down slowly on the back corner of the flat-top griddle for hours in their own natural sugars and beef fat juices until they reach a dark, jammy, sweet consistency.
*   The worker drops a two-ounce scoop of these steaming onions directly onto the center of the spread, seals the boat with a sheet of wax paper, and drops it into the guest's red dining room tray or takeout bag alongside a mandatory plastic fork.

By repurposing the burger griddle as a high-speed steam chamber and standardizing the 2-slice cheese layout, In-N-Out successfully executes gourmet, made-to-order loaded fries at drive-thru speeds without slowing down burger production.
